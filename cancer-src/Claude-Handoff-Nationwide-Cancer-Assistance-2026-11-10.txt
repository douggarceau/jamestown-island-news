NATIONWIDE CANCER ASSISTANCE
72 programs, benefits and practical assistance routes
Official-source review: November 10, 2026 (America/New_York)

PURPOSE
Help people in active cancer treatment, recovering after treatment, living with lasting effects, and their caregivers identify money, bill relief and practical services. Entries distinguish cash benefits, grants, bills paid to vendors, donated services, insurance coverage and navigation.

SCOPE AND VERIFICATION
This is a broad national resource guide assembled from official agency, nonprofit, hospital-program and program-partner pages. It includes national programs, state-administered benefit pathways, and one clearly labeled New England entry useful to Rhode Island residents. It does not certify availability in every ZIP code or every state-administered program.
Public program information was reviewed on the date above. No applications were submitted, providers contacted by telephone, or personal eligibility decisions obtained. Where direct page retrieval was blocked, official indexed text supplied the stated evidence. Dynamic grant status, service capacity and eligibility must be checked at application.
An award maximum is a ceiling, not an entitlement. Navigation organizations help find assistance but do not necessarily provide cash. Treatment, recovery, remission and active surveillance have different definitions across funders.

GOOD STARTING POINTS FOR SOMEONE RECOVERING FROM PROSTATE CANCER
- ZERO360 (#61): prostate-specific insurance, bill and disability navigation.
- CancerCare navigation (#63), plus its post-treatment group (#56) if within the 18-month window.
- Hospital assistance/Dollar For (#8-9) for remaining hospital debt.
- Survivorship rehabilitation and LIVESTRONG at the YMCA (#52-53) for recovery needs.
- SSDI/SSI screening (#1-2) when current functional limitations and the relevant work/financial rules apply.
- General food, home-energy and housing benefits (#35-40) when financially eligible.
- ZERO peer support and Reel Recovery (#54, #57) for connection after treatment.

DISABILITY: WHAT MAKES AN APPLICATION USEFUL
Document what cancer and treatment effects prevent you from doing: sustained attendance, sitting/standing/walking, lifting, concentration and other work functions. Include relevant medical records, treatment dates, clinical assessments and expected duration. Remission does not automatically rule out disability, and cancer history alone does not establish it. SSA evaluates residual impairments when treatment has been effective.
For someone who has stopped working, ask SSA to examine insured status and disability onset. For a denial, follow the notice: SSA generally requires an appeal within 60 days after receipt.
Sources:
https://www.ssa.gov/disability/professionals/bluebook/13.00-NeoplasticDiseases-Malignant-Adult.htm
https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/disability/qualify.html
https://www.ssa.gov/appeals/appeals_process.html

APPLICATION PREPARATION
Prepare diagnosis/treatment confirmation, dates of treatment completion and ongoing care, the specific bill or expense, insurance information, household size/current income documentation, and the care team's contact. Each program may request additional evidence.
Ask the oncology social worker to screen for grants that require professional submission. Cancer Cartel, Family Reach, Joe Andruzzi and several transplant/pediatric programs use professional or navigator routes.
Suggested request: "Please screen me for financial assistance, disability benefits, hospital bill reductions, medication help, transportation, home services and survivorship rehabilitation. Please check programs that accept people after treatment, and tell me which applications your office must submit."

RESOURCE DIRECTORY

INCOME, DISABILITY AND WORK PROTECTIONS

1. Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)
Help: Monthly income when a qualifying impairment prevents substantial work.
Eligibility: Requires enough recent covered work and SSA's disability standard: inability to perform substantial gainful work expected to last at least 12 consecutive months or result in death. A cancer diagnosis alone is insufficient. Retirement from work does not itself establish disability; ask SSA about the date last insured.
After treatment: Possible after successful cancer treatment when documented residual impairments still meet SSA's rules. No need to wait 12 months to apply if the duration requirement is expected to be met.
Amount/cost: Based on the worker's earnings record; generally a five-month waiting period.
Apply/contact: Apply through SSA; 800-772-1213. Request an assessment of work credits, insured status and potential onset date.
Status: Program described on official website; individual eligibility and capacity not confirmed.
Reviewed: 2026-11-10
Official sources:
https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/disability/qualify.html
https://www.ssa.gov/disability/professionals/bluebook/13.00-NeoplasticDiseases-Malignant-Adult.htm

2. Supplemental Security Income (SSI)
Help: Monthly means-tested cash assistance for basic needs.
Eligibility: Low income and limited countable resources, plus qualifying disability/blindness or age 65+. No prior work record required. Ordinary countable-resource limits are $2,000 individual/$3,000 couple; exclusions and household rules apply.
After treatment: Possible after treatment if disability or age-based eligibility and financial criteria are met.
Amount/cost: Varies with countable income, living arrangements and possible state supplements.
Apply/contact: Apply through SSA; 800-772-1213.
Status: Program described on official website; individual eligibility and capacity not confirmed.
Reviewed: 2026-11-10
Official sources:
https://www.ssa.gov/ssi/eligibility

3. SSA Compassionate Allowances
Help: Faster disability claim identification and processing for listed serious conditions.
Eligibility: Specific diagnoses/severity are required. The prostate entry is hormone-refractory disease or visceral metastases; it does not cover every prostate cancer diagnosis.
After treatment: Only if the exact qualifying condition applies; other disability evaluation remains available for lasting impairments.
Amount/cost: Not an additional grant or separate benefit, and not a universal waiver of payment waiting rules.
Apply/contact: Tell SSA the exact diagnosis and provide pathology, staging and treatment records.
Status: Program described on official website; individual eligibility and capacity not confirmed.
Reviewed: 2026-11-10
Official sources:
https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/links/0423022080
https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/disability/qualify.html

4. Employer or private short-term/long-term disability coverage
Help: Income replacement under an existing disability policy.
Eligibility: Coverage must apply under the actual policy; medical definition, coverage dates, exclusions, filing deadlines and offsets differ. Request the Summary Plan Description or policy from the plan administrator.
After treatment: Possible if treatment effects meet the policy definition, even after active treatment ends.
Amount/cost: Policy-specific; not the same program as SSDI.
Apply/contact: Contact the insurer/benefits administrator. DOL EBSA assistance: 866-444-3272.
Status: Program described on official website; individual eligibility and capacity not confirmed.
Reviewed: 2026-11-10

5. State paid medical leave or temporary disability; paid family leave
Help: Partial wage replacement for qualifying workers, and in some states caregivers.
Eligibility: State-specific employment, wage and contribution rules; these benefits are not available under one nationwide policy. A retired person without qualifying covered employment should not assume eligibility.
After treatment: May cover a medically certified recovery period or qualifying caregiving leave.
Amount/cost: State- and earnings-specific.
Apply/contact: Use your state's labor agency. Ask separately about your own medical leave and caregiver leave.
Status: Program described on official website; individual eligibility and capacity not confirmed.
Reviewed: 2026-11-10
Official sources:
https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fmla/workplace-protections-for-individuals-cancer

6. VA disability compensation and cancer-related health benefits
Help: Tax-free disability compensation and potentially VA health care for service-connected disease.
Eligibility: Military service and connection/presumption requirements apply. Prostate cancer is on VA's Agent Orange presumptive list for qualifying exposure; PACT Act coverage depends on applicable service/exposure rules.
After treatment: Ask VA to assess ongoing residuals and current entitlement; a history of cancer does not by itself fix the payment level.
Amount/cost: Based on VA rating and circumstances.
Apply/contact: VA: 800-698-2411; apply through VA or an accredited veterans representative.
Status: Program described on official website; individual eligibility and capacity not confirmed.
Reviewed: 2026-11-10

7. FMLA leave and ADA workplace accommodations
Help: Job protection, continued health coverage under qualifying FMLA leave, and reasonable workplace adjustments.
Eligibility: Employee/employer eligibility and legal standards apply. FMLA generally provides up to 12 workweeks of unpaid protected leave; it is not a cash benefit. ADA accommodations depend on need and undue-hardship rules.
After treatment: Can remain relevant during remission and for lasting treatment limitations.
Amount/cost: Protection rather than a grant; paid leave may run alongside it.
Apply/contact: Employer HR; DOL Wage and Hour Division: 866-487-9243. Triage Cancer can help explain options.
Status: Program described on official website; individual eligibility and capacity not confirmed.

Eligibility: Tax-exempt hospitals must maintain written assistance policies. Income, household size and hardship criteria differ; having insurance does not automatically prevent help. Separate physician bills may not be covered.
After treatment: Yes: ask about outstanding bills even after treatment and, where relevant, bills in collections.
Amount/cost: May reduce or eliminate qualifying balances; no national dollar amount.
Apply/contact: Ask the hospital billing office for its financial assistance application, plain-language policy and list of covered providers.
Status: Program described on official website; individual eligibility and capacity not confirmed.
