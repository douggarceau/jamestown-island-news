Foulkes Elected Governor as Democrats Sweep Rhode Island's Statewide Offices

The former CVS executive beat independent Ken Block and Republican Aaron Guckian by about 30 points. Kim Ahern becomes attorney general, and the state's congressional delegation returns intact.

Helena Buonanno Foulkes will be Rhode Island's next governor. The Associated Press called the race for the Democrat at about 8:35 p.m. on Election Night, Nov. 3, less than an hour after the polls closed.

With the count nearly complete, unofficial results show Foulkes with about 57.9 percent of the vote. Independent Ken Block finished second with about 27.9 percent, and Republican Aaron Guckian third with about 13.2 percent. Two other independents, C.D. Reynolds and Jasjit Gotra, each drew less than 1 percent.

A two-month climb

Foulkes's path to the State House began in September, when she defeated Gov. Dan McKee in the Democratic primary. She carried all 39 cities and towns and won about 62.5 percent of the vote. McKee became the first Rhode Island governor to lose a primary since Bruce Sundlun in 1994. Foulkes had lost to McKee in the 2022 primary by about 3 points.

Foulkes, 62, has never held elected office. She was a longtime executive at CVS and later chief executive of Hudson's Bay Company. She will be the second woman to serve as Rhode Island's governor.

Election night

Foulkes spoke to supporters in Providence on Tuesday night. She told the crowd that voters had made a choice about what kind of state they want, and she said she would work for every Rhode Islander, including those who did not vote for her.

After her speech, she told reporters she will run a national search for a new director of the state Department of Transportation, which has faced sharp criticism over the closure of the Washington Bridge.

Block, speaking to his supporters in downtown Providence, said the state needs the new governor to succeed. He also said Rhode Island should do more to reduce the role of money in its elections.

The rest of the ballot

Democrats won all five statewide offices. Lt. Gov. Sabina Matos won another term over Republican John Loughlin. Kim Ahern, a former prosecutor, was elected attorney general over Republican Alan Gordon; she succeeds Peter Neronha, who could not run again. General Treasurer James Diossa was reelected, and Secretary of State Gregg Amore was reelected without opposition.

In Washington races, U.S. Sen. Jack Reed defeated Republican Raymond McKay, and U.S. Reps. Gabe Amo and Seth Magaziner kept their seats.

What happens next

Turnout was on track to roughly match the 2022 midterm, with nearly 40 percent of voters casting ballots early or by mail. The state Board of Elections is expected to certify results before Thanksgiving, after any recount requests and an audit of the ballot machines. Foulkes takes office on Jan. 4, 2027.
