What Should You Hand Out This Halloween? A Jamestown Guide to Treats

The candies Rhode Island buys most, lower-sugar ideas and the teal pumpkin treats that let kids with food allergies join in.

Every porch light on the island faces the same question this week: what will the kids actually be happy to get?

What Rhode Island is buying

According to CandyStore.com's annual state-by-state report, which is based on the bulk retailer's own sales and data from candy makers and distributors, Rhode Island's top three Halloween candies this year are Hershey's assorted Mini Bars, M&M's and Double Bubble gum.

Nationally, the same report ranks M&M's first, followed by Sour Patch Kids, Skittles, Reese's Cups and NERDS Gummy Clusters.

Lower-sugar ideas

These are our suggestions, not a ranking: individually sealed applesauce pouches, mini pretzel bags, small packs of whole-grain crackers, freeze-dried fruit and little boxes of raisins. Lower-sugar doesn't mean allergy-safe, so read the labels.

Treats that aren't food

A teal pumpkin on your step is the signal, through Food Allergy Research & Education's Teal Pumpkin Project, that your house has non-food treats, so children with food allergies can trick-or-treat too.

FARE suggests glow sticks and light-up jewelry, pencils, crayons and markers, Halloween erasers and pencil toppers, bubbles, mini Slinkies, bouncy balls, spider rings, vampire fangs, stickers and mini notepads. A Halloween pencil and eraser in a little bag makes a handy school-supply treat. Skip anything with small detachable pieces for the youngest kids, and keep non-food treats in a separate bowl from the candy.

Where to stock up on the island

McQuade's Marketplace at 6 Clarke Street in the village is the island's grocery store. Call (401) 423-0873 to check the candy aisle before Saturday.
