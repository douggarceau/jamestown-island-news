Harlem's Cultural Giants Return to the Screen at the Jamestown Arts Center

newportFILM's screening of Once Upon a Time in Harlem tonight is sold out. Here's the story behind the film, and what's next at the Arts Center.

newportFILM brings Once Upon a Time in Harlem to the Jamestown Arts Center on Thursday, Oct. 29. The screening is sold out.

The film

In 1972, filmmaker William Greaves filmed a party at Duke Ellington's townhouse in Harlem, where surviving figures of the Harlem Renaissance gathered to talk about the movement. Among them were the painter Aaron Douglas, the writers Arna Bontemps and Richard Bruce Nugent, the musicians Eubie Blake and Noble Sissle, and the photographer James Van Der Zee.

The footage sat unfinished for 50 years. After William Greaves died, his son David Greaves returned to it and completed the 100-minute documentary, which premiered at the 2026 Sundance Film Festival.

Tonight's program

The evening opens with a 6 p.m. reception, with beer and wine for a suggested donation, followed by the film at 7. Afterward, April H. Brown and S. Kai Cameron, co-directors of the Langston Hughes Community Poetry Reading, lead a conversation. Tickets were $25.

Also coming up at the Arts Center

Wednesday, Nov. 4, 6 to 7 p.m.: photographer Onne van der Wal presents The Family of Man, portraits from his travels around the world. Pay what you can.

Saturday, Nov. 7: Italian Wannabe, a 2026 documentary about food and community directed by Steve Dabal. Doors open at 6:30 p.m. and the film starts at 7. Tickets are $15.

Friday, Nov. 20, 5:30 to 7:30 p.m.: opening reception for the annual Members' Show. No admission charge.

The Jamestown Arts Center is at 18 Valley Street.
