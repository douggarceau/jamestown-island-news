Imprint and Playback at the Jamestown Arts Center, Through Nov. 7

Seven RISD Movement Lab fellows show motion-capture, augmented-reality and sound work at 18 Valley Street, Jamestown, through Nov. 7.

The Jamestown Arts Center's current exhibition, Imprint and Playback, closes on Saturday, November 7, which leaves little time to see work by seven artists who took part in the Movement Lab Fellowship at the Rhode Island School of Design between 2023 and 2026.

The show

The exhibition was curated by Africanus Okokon and L.A. Samuelson, and the artists are Azadeh Ahmadi, Patrick Buhr, Hunter Whitaker-Morrow, Kameron Neal, L.A. Samuelson, Maxime Cavajani and Melisa Achoko-Allela. Their work explores how beings, materials, ideas and memories travel across time, landscape, narrative, archive, communication and performance. Human and nonhuman bodies are central, and many pieces shift form or embrace instability to draw the viewer in.

Three works stand out in the gallery's description. Achoko-Allela's Songa is an augmented-reality installation with sound that builds a motion-capture database from everyday gestures in African orature. Whitaker-Morrow's Untitled (Realtime System) takes apart Little House on the Prairie through a live, constantly changing "volatile sensorial system". And Buhr's We move in circles to feel the center uses hand-tracked animation to connect viewers' bodies without physical contact.

Visiting

The Arts Center is at 18 Valley Street, Jamestown, RI 02835. Gallery hours are Wednesday to Saturday, 11 a.m. to 3 p.m., or by appointment, and the facility is fully wheelchair accessible. Questions can be directed to (401) 560-0979.

What comes next

The Center has a lively calendar after the show closes. Photographer Onne van der Wal presents a program of portraits from his travels around the world, The Family of Man, on Wednesday, November 4, from 6 to 7 p.m.m. that is open to all at no charge. The Holiday Arts Market follows on December 4 and 5, also at no charge.

For islanders, it is a short trip to see the kind of experimental work usually found in the city.
