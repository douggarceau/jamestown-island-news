Jamestown Votes Today on $3 Million Housing Bond and Town Leadership

Question 6 asks whether the town may borrow up to $3 million for affordable housing, as voters also pick five councilors, two School Committee members and the moderator.

JAMESTOWN — A decision about how to finance more affordable housing on the island is before Jamestown voters today, alongside the selection of five Town Council members, two School Committee members and the town moderator. The local ballot places housing investment and municipal leadership at the center of Election Day.

The housing bond

Question 6 asks voters whether Jamestown should be authorized to issue up to $3 million in bonds and notes for affordable housing projects. Approval would give the town borrowing authority to acquire property and develop affordable housing, including on land it already owns. Rejection would withhold the borrowing authority proposed in this question.

Housing materials included in the Town Council’s October 19 meeting packet describe the challenge in distinctly local terms: helping families and people who work in Jamestown find housing they can afford while respecting the island’s limited land, drinking-water supply and wastewater capacity. The materials argue that local funding could help the town compete for outside grants and fill financing gaps that otherwise prevent projects from moving forward. Those are the town’s stated objectives; passage of the bond would not itself guarantee additional grants.

One property identified in those materials is 11 Knowles Court, where a conceptual proposal calls for 10 affordable housing units. The October packet describes a town-owned site near public transportation and existing apartments, with further review, permitting and contracting still ahead. That distinction matters: the referendum concerns financing authority, while the design and approval of individual projects involve additional steps.

What it would cost

For taxpayers, the financial commitment extends beyond the dollar figure printed on the ballot. The authorizing legislation requires Jamestown to provide for principal and interest payments when other funding is insufficient, and permits bond maturities extending as long as 30 years. The $3 million figure is the borrowing ceiling; total repayment would also include interest. The eventual cost would depend on how much the town borrows and the financing terms established when debt is issued.

The same legislation gives the Town Council responsibility for authorizing bond sales and carrying out the housing projects and contracts. That connects the borrowing question to another decision voters face today: who will sit on the council as those choices are made.

Town Council

Six candidates appear on the ballot for five two-year Town Council seats. They are Democrats Mary G. Glackin, Kirstin Renetta Reveron, Nicholas J. Insana, Marisa A. Quinn and Richard J. Smith, and independent Robert M. Bailey. Voters may select up to five candidates.

School Committee and moderator

For School Committee, Democrats Christian Cowan and Sharon Rallis are the two listed candidates for two four-year seats. Bruce W. McIntyre, a Democrat, is the sole listed candidate for a two-year term as town moderator. Write-in spaces are available in each of these local contests.

Also waiting for the next council

Beyond housing, the winners inherit island business already in motion, according to minutes of the Oct. 5 council meeting. The council set a public hearing for Nov. 2 on zoning changes that would allow a fire or police station or substation, a step toward a possible north-end fire station. It approved moving the town's trash and recycling contract from Island Rubbish to Eastern Waste Services, which says it will honor the contract through October 2028. And it accepted a $147,396 state road-paving grant that requires a town match of $294,792 by the end of 2029.

The town is also buying 73 Pemberton Ave. with a federal Community Development Block Grant to offer as an affordable home, with the closing moved to July 1, 2027. Senior services are moving back into the renovated center on West Street in early November.

Before you go

Jamestown’s polls are open from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. today. Residents should confirm their assigned polling location through the official Rhode Island Voter Information Center at vote.sos.ri.gov before heading out. Bring an accepted photo ID; voters without one may cast a provisional ballot. Residents still holding a completed mail ballot should use an official drop box if it cannot reach election officials by the 8 p.m. deadline. Official ballot-return information is at elections.ri.gov.

Official results will be posted by the Rhode Island Board of Elections at electionresults.ri.gov after the polls close. We'll report the island's results when they're in.
