Jamestown Election: Quinn Leads Council Vote, Final Seat Separated by 36 Votes, Housing Bond Approved

Reveron stands 36 votes ahead of Glackin for the fifth council seat, and voters approve borrowing up to $3 million for affordable housing, in unofficial results.

Jamestown voters chose five Town Council members and approved borrowing up to $3 million for affordable housing in the Tuesday, Nov. 3, general election. Marisa A. Quinn led the council field. The closest contest was for the fifth seat, where Kirstin Renetta Reveron stands 36 votes ahead of Mary G. Glackin.

These figures come from the Rhode Island Board of Elections' Jamestown results, last updated Wednesday, Nov. 4, at 8:02 p.m. They remain unofficial until the board certifies them.

The Town Council

Jamestown elects five council members townwide to two-year terms. Each voter may choose up to five candidates, and the five with the most votes win.

If the order holds through certification, the council will have four Democrats and one independent. Quinn finished 100 votes ahead of Insana, and Smith finished 184 ahead of Bailey. The bottom of the group was much tighter: Bailey leads Reveron by 12 votes, and Reveron leads Glackin by 36.

The race recorded 11,877 selections, including write-ins, from 3,305 ballots, because voters could choose several candidates. A percentage on the state's results page, such as Quinn's 18.72%, is a share of all council selections, not the share of voters who chose that candidate.

Where the fifth seat was decided

The state divides Jamestown's results between precinct 1501, at the Recreation Center, and precinct 1502, at Lawn Avenue School. These totals count every voting method assigned to each precinct, not only people who voted inside those buildings on Election Day.

Quinn led precinct 1501 and Insana led precinct 1502. Reveron led Glackin by 43 votes in precinct 1501, while Glackin led Reveron by 7 in precinct 1502, which produced the 36-vote townwide margin.

Bailey received the most Election Day votes, narrowly ahead of Quinn, while Quinn led early voting and mail ballots. Reveron's margin over Glackin came from 17 more Election Day votes and 22 more early votes, offset by Glackin's 3-vote edge among mail ballots.

The $3 million housing question

Question 6 asked voters to authorize the town to issue up to $3 million in bonds and notes for affordable housing, including buying property and developing housing on land the town already owns. It passed by 563 votes, and both precincts supported it.

The question recorded 3,145 votes, 160 fewer than the town's total ballots cast. The published results do not say why those ballots show no recorded vote on the question.

School Committee and moderator

Christian Cowan and Sharon Rallis, both Democrats, were the only names on the ballot for two School Committee seats. Bruce W. McIntyre, a Democrat, was the only name on the ballot for town moderator.

Turnout

The state lists 3,305 ballots cast by Jamestown's 5,105 registered voters, a turnout of about 65 percent.

These results are unofficial until the Board of Elections certifies them. Updated totals will be posted on the board's results site.
