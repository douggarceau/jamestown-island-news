Coalition to Meet Nov. 9 on Keeping Newport Hospital's Birthing Center Open

Seven months after Brown University Health said the center would stay open if $4.9 million a year in new money is found, local lawmakers will update residents at the Newport Public Library.

A coalition working to keep the Noreen Stonor Drexel Birthing Center at Newport Hospital open will hold a public meeting at 5:30 p.m. Monday, Nov. 9, at the Newport Public Library, 300 Spring St., What'sUpNewp reported. The meeting is open to residents, families, health care workers and community leaders.

The Newport Birthing Center Coalition is led by Rep. Lauren H. Carson, a Democrat who represents House District 75 in Newport, and Sen. Dawn Euer, a Democrat whose Senate District 13 covers Newport and Jamestown. The birthing center serves families across Newport County, including Jamestown.

How the center was saved, for now

On April 3, Brown University Health, the hospital's parent system, announced that the birthing center would stay open. The decision followed a review by the consulting firm Kaufman Hall and advice from a community advisory panel the hospital formed the previous November.

The Kaufman Hall review found the center provided safe, quality care, with obstetric, pediatric and anesthesia coverage around the clock. It also raised concerns about whether staffing could be sustained and how reliably the unit could operate with a small number of births.

The numbers

Brown Health said the center needs about $4.9 million a year in new revenue to keep operating, money it said would have to come from state funding and private giving. The health system said at the time that it would launch an aggressive effort to secure the funding. The report did not say how much has been raised since April or whether the gap has been closed.

What's on the agenda

According to What'sUpNewp, the meeting will include an update on the center and the campaign to keep it open, and a report on the coalition's talks with leaders of Brown Health and Newport Hospital. The coalition also plans to present recommendations for expanding the center into what it calls a first-class maternity facility for all of Newport County, and to discuss priorities for the 2027 General Assembly session, which begins in January.

Carson and Euer said the coalition is committed to preserving the center and to making it a model for maternity care in Rhode Island.

What it means for residents

The center serves expectant families from across Newport County. Its future has been uncertain since last fall, when the hospital formed a community advisory panel to help weigh options. Brown Health's April decision kept the unit running, but tied its long-term future to finding new money each year rather than to the hospital's existing budget.

That makes the coalition's work with state lawmakers central. Carson and Euer both represent Newport in the General Assembly, and Euer's district also includes Jamestown, so residents of both communities are represented directly in the coalition's leadership. The meeting is a chance for families and health care workers to hear where talks with the hospital stand and to weigh in on the group's plans.

What comes next

Because Brown Health has tied the center's future to new state money, the coalition's priorities for the 2027 legislative session are likely to be a focus. The General Assembly convenes in January, and any state funding would be considered as part of the next state budget.
