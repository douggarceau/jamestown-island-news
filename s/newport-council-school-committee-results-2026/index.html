Newport Elects Holder, Smyth, Dring and Cullen; Fifth Place Falls 46 Votes Short

The new City Council includes one new ward councilor, and the School Committee will see six new members after most incumbents did not run.

Newport voters returned Mayor Charles M. Holder Jr. and Councilor Stephanie Smyth to the City Council on Nov. 3 and added School Committee Chair James Dring and Elizabeth E. Cullen to the four at-large seats, according to unofficial results reported by What'sUpNewp. Taryn Mazza finished fifth, 46 votes behind Cullen.

All of Newport's polling places had reported by 10:04 p.m. The results remain unofficial until they are certified.

The ward races

In Ward 1, incumbent Ellen M. Pinnock defeated John Michael, 920 to 715. In Ward 2, Emily Conklin won the seat, defeating John M. Walker 1,455 to 1,188. Incumbent Lynn Underwood Ceglie had filed for re-election in June but was not among the candidates in the reported results. In Ward 3, David R. Carlin III ran unopposed and received 1,735 votes.

The mayor of Newport is not elected directly. The seven council members choose the mayor from among the four at-large councilors, What'sUpNewp has reported.

A new School Committee

The School Committee will look very different. When filing closed in June, six of its seven members did not seek another term on the committee. Dring and Cullen ran for City Council instead, and four others did not file for any office. Nine candidates ran for the seven seats.

Robert J. Leary, the only incumbent on the ballot, kept his seat. Brian Rochelle finished eighth, 273 votes short. What'sUpNewp noted that four of the seven seats went to candidates who had been the focus of competing endorsement campaigns.

State and federal races in the city

Sen. Dawn Euer, whose District 13 covers Newport and Jamestown, and Rep. Lauren H. Carson of House District 75 were both unopposed. In Newport, Gov.-elect Helena Foulkes drew 57.5 percent, independent Ken Block 27.9 percent and Republican Aaron Guckian 13.2 percent.

In the federal races, Sen. Jack Reed drew 66.6 percent in Newport and Rep. Gabe Amo 68.4 percent; the Associated Press called both statewide races for the incumbents. Lt. Gov. Sabina Matos, Secretary of State Gregg Amore, Attorney General-elect Kim Ahern and General Treasurer James Diossa were also leading in the city's count.

What it means for residents

Three of the four at-large councilors are familiar faces in city government: Holder and Smyth return, and Dring moves over from leading the School Committee. Cullen also comes to the council from the School Committee. On the ward side, Conklin is the only newcomer. On the School Committee, Leary will be the lone returning member, alongside six people taking a seat for the first time. That committee oversees Newport's public schools and their budget.

The close finish for the fourth at-large seat means a small number of votes decided the makeup of the council. Final, certified totals could shift slightly from the unofficial count.

What comes next

Once the results are certified, the new council and School Committee will be sworn in, and the council will choose the mayor and vice chair from among its members.
