From Ocean Racing Champion to Acclaimed Photographer: Onne van der Wal Comes to Jamestown

The marine photographer presents “The Family of Man” at the Jamestown Arts Center, 18 Valley Street, Wednesday, Nov. 4, 6 to 7 p.m.

Internationally acclaimed marine photographer Onne van der Wal will present “The Family of Man” at the Jamestown Arts Center, 18 Valley Street, on Wednesday, November 4, from 6 to 7 p.m. Known for capturing spectacular sailing scenes in some of the world's most demanding waters, van der Wal will turn his attention to another side of his work: portraits of people encountered during his travels. The presentation takes inspiration from Edward Steichen's landmark 1955 photography exhibition, which explored the experiences and emotions shared by people around the world.

Born in the Netherlands and raised in Hout Bay, South Africa, van der Wal grew up in a seafaring family and learned to sail on his grandfather's boat. He eventually became a professional sailor and joined the crew of the Dutch maxi-yacht Flyer II for the 1981–82 Whitbread Round the World Race. Serving as both engineer and bowman, he helped the yacht win all four legs of the competition. His responsibilities included handling sails at the dangerous forward end of the vessel, often while being battered by heavy seas.

Van der Wal's photography career began almost by accident. During preparations for the Whitbread race, the yacht stopped in Marblehead, Massachusetts, where he showed his photographs to SAIL magazine editor Keith Taylor. Impressed by the images, Taylor arranged for him to document the round-the-world voyage. Van der Wal carried an Olympus camera and worked with a limited supply of film, sending exposed rolls from ports along the route for processing. His extraordinary images earned him the February 1982 cover of SAIL magazine.

The assignments required considerable physical courage. Van der Wal photographed from positions most people would consider unthinkable, including the top of a yacht's mast and the outer end of a spinnaker pole above the ocean. His technical knowledge of sail handling, rigging and racing conditions allowed him to anticipate moments that photographers without his sailing background might miss. He continued racing professionally for several years before establishing himself as a full-time photographer in 1987.

His photographs have appeared in major sailing publications around the world. In 2013, Rizzoli published his large-format book Sailing, featuring approximately 200 color photographs. His more recent book, Jamestown, RI, celebrates the island and its coastal scenery.

Wednesday's presentation offers Jamestown audiences an opportunity to discover the human subjects behind a photographer best known for boats, waves and dramatic seascapes. Admission and ticket information are available through the Jamestown Arts Center's official event page.
