Revolution Wind Reaches Full Power Off the Rhode Island Coast

The 704-megawatt, 65-turbine wind farm is now fully delivering electricity to the regional grid, about 14 months after a federal stop-work order paused construction.

Revolution Wind, the 65-turbine offshore wind farm about 15 miles south of the Rhode Island coast, has reached full commercial operation and is delivering power to the regional grid, according to Rep. Gabe Amo's office and the Conservation Law Foundation, as reported by What'sUpNewp on Nov. 5.

The 704-megawatt project is a joint venture of the Danish developer Ørsted and Skyborn Renewables. At full output it can supply enough electricity for roughly 350,000 homes and businesses in Rhode Island and Connecticut. The site is also about 32 miles southeast of the Connecticut coast.

A stop and a restart

The project began sending its first power to the grid in March, What'sUpNewp reported. Its path to completion was not smooth. In August 2025, with construction nearly finished, the Trump administration issued a stop-work order that halted the work. A federal judge lifted the order in September 2025, and construction resumed. Full operation came about 14 months after the stop.

What supporters said

Amo, a Democrat whose district includes Newport County, said the project supported more than 1,000 union jobs during construction and is now fully running. He described it as an example of Rhode Island's ocean-based economy at work, and criticized the administration's earlier actions against the project.

Anxhela Mile, a staff attorney at the Conservation Law Foundation, said offshore wind projects like Revolution Wind show what New England can do with energy produced in the region. She said they create jobs and provide a steady source of electricity as fossil fuel costs rise. The foundation noted that the region's ocean winds are strongest in winter, when demand for power is high.

What it means for ratepayers

The electricity is sold under 20-year contracts with utilities in Rhode Island and Connecticut. Connecticut's Department of Energy and Environmental Protection estimates the project will save the region about $500 million in electricity costs by 2028, according to the report.

Supporters say long-term contracts like these help steady power supplies for Rhode Island households and businesses at a time of rising fossil fuel costs, which the Conservation Law Foundation cited as a key benefit. The project is also one of the largest single sources of new electricity serving the state, with enough output for hundreds of thousands of customers across two states.

The bigger picture

Revolution Wind is the third offshore wind farm delivering power to the Northeast grid, joining Vineyard Wind off Massachusetts, with 62 turbines, and South Fork Wind, a dozen turbines less than 20 miles from Block Island that serve Long Island. The Conservation Law Foundation said the three together displace more than 3 million tons of carbon dioxide a year.

Rhode Island was home to the nation's first offshore wind farm, the five-turbine Block Island Wind Farm, which began operating in 2016.

What comes next

With the turbines fully running, the project now enters long-term operation under its utility contracts. Its developers and state officials have not announced further milestones.
