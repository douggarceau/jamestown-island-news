Electric Bills Could Drop Up to 75% Under New Rhode Island Energy Discount

Five income-based discount levels from 10% to 75% replace the current 25% and 30% discounts, starting in early 2027.

Thousands of Rhode Island households could pay far less for electricity next year. Rhode Island Energy announced on Monday, Nov. 9, a new Enhanced Electric Discount Rate that will cut eligible electric charges by 10% to 75%, depending on household income.

The program replaces the utility's current discount, which has two levels of 25% and 30%, with five income-based levels. The deepest discounts go to the households with the lowest incomes. Natural gas discount rates are not changing.

When it starts

State regulators authorized Rhode Island Energy to launch the new discounts in January 2027 but did not set a specific date, according to Rhode Island Current. The utility's announcement says it is working to put the program in place in early 2027 and that there are no immediate bill changes. Don't expect the new discount on November or December bills.

How much you could save

Rhode Island Energy sets the discount by household income as a share of the federal poverty level.

At the 75% level, $200 in eligible charges would drop to $50, about $1,800 a year if charges stayed the same. These are illustrations, not guaranteed savings: the discount applies to eligible charges, and the level depends on income and eligibility.

Income alone doesn't qualify a household. You first have to be eligible through one of the assistance programs below.

Who qualifies

You may qualify if you take part in at least one of these programs: the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP), the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), Supplemental Security Income (SSI), Medicaid, Rhode Island Works, or General Public Assistance.

Once you're eligible through one of those programs, the Rhode Island Department of Human Services works out your household income as a share of the poverty level and assigns your discount level. DHS shares that with Rhode Island Energy, which sends a letter confirming your enrollment and discount.

Already getting a discount?

Most of the roughly 33,000 customers already enrolled in the current program will move to the new discounts automatically when the program begins, Rhode Island Energy told Rhode Island Current. Some may be asked to confirm information such as name, address and income. Rhode Island Energy says current customers will be notified in the coming weeks.

To keep the discount, Rhode Island Energy says the account holder must be a residential customer with the bill in their name, be the head of household or principal wage earner, and take part in at least one of the qualifying programs.

How to apply or check

Start with Rhode Island Energy's Enhanced Electric Discount Rate page, which has the full income chart. If you're not in a qualifying program yet, your local Community Action Program (CAP) agency can help you apply for LIHEAP, and the Department of Human Services handles SNAP, Medicaid, SSI, Rhode Island Works and General Public Assistance.

Other help still available

The new discount doesn't replace other help. LIHEAP, payment-assistance programs and energy-efficiency and weatherization programs remain separate, each with its own eligibility rules.

Why it matters

More than 114,000 Rhode Island Energy customers were behind on their electric bills this fall, Rhode Island Current reported, and more than 4,000 had their power shut off for unpaid bills as of September. The new program aims to keep eligible customers' electricity costs to no more than 3% of their income. Its deepest savings go to households well below the poverty line, so it's worth checking your level rather than assuming your current discount is the most you can get.
