Voters Approve $450 Million in Bonds, Reject Two for the First Time Since 2006

Rhode Islanders backed borrowing for higher education, housing and the environment but turned down economic development and a State History Center.

Rhode Island voters approved three of the five statewide bond questions on the Nov. 3 ballot, authorizing $450 million in borrowing for public colleges, housing and environmental projects, according to unofficial Board of Elections results reported by Rhode Island Current. They rejected the other two, worth a combined $150 million, for economic development and a new State History Center.

It is the first time since 2006 that Rhode Island voters have turned down a state bond, according to Rhode Island Current and NBC 10. That year, voters rejected a $4 million bond for Fort Adams in Newport. Every bond put before voters in the two decades since had passed. This year's $600 million package was the largest ever placed on a Rhode Island ballot, Rhode Island Current reported.

What the approved money pays for

The largest piece, Question 1, includes $165 million for a new Integrated Health Building at the University of Rhode Island's Kingston campus to serve nursing, pharmacy and health sciences programs. It also provides $60 million for a 44,000-square-foot Workforce Innovation Center at the Community College of Rhode Island's Knight Campus in Warwick, focused on trades such as plumbing, heating and welding, and $50 million to renovate Rhode Island College's Adams Library and create a student success and career center.

Question 2's $120 million for housing matches a bond voters approved in 2024. At least $25 million is set aside for homeownership housing, and up to $10 million could go to public housing. Rhode Island Current reported that projects are expected to begin in 2028.

Question 5 includes $25 million for the Resilient Rhody Infrastructure Fund, which helps cities and towns deal with flooding and coastal erosion, $12 million for state parks, beaches and campgrounds, $7 million for Narragansett Bay and watershed restoration, $3 million each for brownfield cleanup, farmland preservation and open space, and $2 million for local recreation and marine projects such as boat launches.

What voters turned down

Question 3 would have spent $55 million on site work at places such as the Quonset Business Park and Providence's I-195 district, and $45 million on infrastructure for defense, ocean technology and other industries. No specific projects were named. Question 4 would have built a 24,600-square-foot State History Center across from the State House, plus $5 million in historic preservation grants for municipalities.

Secretary of State Gregg Amore, who championed the history center, told NBC 10 he understood the state's economic pressures and competing priorities but was disappointed the project did not pass this year.

Why some voted no

House Minority Leader Michael Chippendale, a Republican, told NBC 10 that many voters who said no were concerned about the debt that comes with bonds. A Middletown voter told Rhode Island Current she rejected Question 3 because its description was not detailed enough.

The cost

Bonds are repaid with interest through future state budgets. Using the voter guide's model of 5 percent interest over 20 years, the three approved measures would cost about $722 million in principal and interest, compared with about $963 million had all five passed, Rhode Island Current reported. Approval does not mean the state borrows the money right away.

What comes next

The results remain unofficial until the Board of Elections certifies them. The state will then decide when to issue the approved bonds as projects move forward.
