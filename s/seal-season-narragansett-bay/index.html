Seal Season Is Almost Here: Where to See Harbor Seals This Winter

Rhode Island's state marine mammal returns to the bay for the cold months, and Save The Bay's seal tours run November through April.

One of the best parts of a Narragansett Bay winter is about to start. Harbor seals, Rhode Island's official state marine mammal, come into the bay in the cooler months, following their food, and haul out on the rocks to rest.

By boat

Save The Bay runs seal tours out of Newport from November through April. The one-hour Seal Tour costs $25, or $20 for members, seniors and kids 3 to 12; children under 3 ride at no charge. A two-hour Seal & Lighthouse Tour with a stop at Rose Island Lighthouse is $60, or $55. The boats head for Citing Rock off Rose Island, where seals often rest, and passengers get binoculars. Book at 401-203-SEAL (7325) and confirm where your trip departs; the tours have been listed from Perrotti Park and Bowen's Wharf.

Save The Bay says its boats keep a safe distance, following Marine Mammal Protection Act guidelines.

From shore

Rome Point in North Kingstown, across the West Passage from the island, is a well-known spot. Rhode Island Monthly suggests going a few hours before low tide, bringing binoculars and keeping your distance; counts there tend to peak in late March and early April.

Give them room

Seals hauled out on the rocks are resting. Keep your distance, keep dogs leashed, and enjoy them through binoculars.
