A Harlem Party and an Italian Kitchen on Screen

Two film nights at the Jamestown Arts Center, Oct. 29 and Nov. 7, both about finding your way home.

The Jamestown Arts Center turns into a movie house twice in the next two weeks, and the two films share a theme: people who build community, lose it, and find their way back.

Oct. 29: Once Upon a Time in Harlem

On Thursday, Oct. 29, newportFILM presents "Once Upon a Time in Harlem" in partnership with the Langston Hughes Community Poetry Reading Committee, according to JAC. Doors open at 6 p.m., and the film starts at 7.

The documentary captures a 1972 party that filmmaker William Greaves threw for living figures of the Harlem Renaissance, many of whom had not seen each other in about 50 years, per the center's listing. Over four hours they reminisce and argue about their place in a changing culture. Shot on 16mm and newly restored, the 100-minute film is credited to David and William Greaves.

Ken Burns called it "The best party I have ever been to," according to the listing. After the screening, April Brown and S. Kai Cameron, co-directors of the poetry reading committee, share reflections. Tickets are sold through newportFILM; street parking is available.

> One film about a reunion, one about a fresh start.

Nov. 7: Italian Wannabe

On Saturday, Nov. 7, doors open at 6:30 p.m. for "Italian Wannabe," with the film at 7, JAC says. It follows chef Bill Disselhorst, who with his wife, Anne, turned a tiny South Pasadena cafe known for fresh bread and shared tables into a neighborhood institution. After a tragedy cost him everything, he returned to the small Italian village where the couple first fell in love with food, looking for a reason to cook again.

The film sold out five screenings at the 2026 Palm Springs International Film Festival and is touring in pop-up screenings, per the center. General admission is $15 through the JAC events store.
